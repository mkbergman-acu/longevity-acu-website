Conditions Treated

According to The World Health Organization, there are over 70 specific diseases or conditions that acupuncture is recognized for based on research from controlled trials conducted up to the year 1999*. Starting at top to the right are excerpts of the generally recognized categories. For more information and to read the original document click here.

* There are still many conditions for which acupuncture may be effective but are not included during the scope of this clinical trial review. Please contact an acupuncturist to see if acupuncture may be right for you.

Pain: Because of the side-effects of long-term drug therapy for pain and the risks of dependence, acupuncture analgesia can be regarded as the method of choice for treating many chronically painful conditions.

Infections: The results of research on the effects of acupuncture treatments that stimulate the immune system suggest that acupuncture may be of use in conjunction with other medical therapies for treating infections.

Neurological disorders: In the neurological field, headaches, migraines and neuralgia are the common painful conditions treated with acupuncture. Strokes and their sequelae are another major indication for acupuncture. Early treatment of paresis after stroke has proved highly effective.

Respiratory disorders: Acupuncture is often used in treating respiratory disorders. Allergic rhinitis is one of the major indications.

Digestive disorders: Epigastric pain is a common symptom in diseases of the stomach, including peptic ulcer, acute and chronic gastritis, and gastric spasm. Acupuncture provides satisfactory relief of epigastric pain-significantly better than injections of anisodamine or morphine plus atropine, as shown in randomized controlled trials.

Blood disorders: Among various blood disorders, leukopenia is the most suitable for acupuncture treatment.

Urogenital disorders: Urinary retention, traumatic retention, renal colic, urinary stones (if they are not too large), Sexual disorders (male & female), acupuncture may also be helpful to patients with chronic prostatitis, and has also been successfully used as a prophylaxis against recurrent lower urinary tract infections.

Gynecological and obstetric disorders: Primary dysmenorrhoea, Premenstrual syndrome, reported to be effective in the treatment of female anovular infertility, effective for female infertility due to inflammatory obstruction of the fallopian tubes, induction of labour, prevention and treatment of morning sickness, abnormal fetal position (last three months of pregnancy), stimulation of milk secretion after childbirth and can be used to treat deficient lactation due to mental lability or depression.

Cardiovascular disorders: Primary hypotension, early essential hypertension, angina pectoris, coronary arteriography, and useful for dilation of the coronary artery.

Psychiatric disorders and mental disturbances: Acupuncture is being increasingly used in psychiatric disorders. The effect of acupuncture on depression (including depressive neurosis and depression following stroke) has been documented repeatedly in controlled studies. There have also been reports that, in controlled trials of schizophrenia treatment, acupuncture might have a better effect than chlorpromazine. Acupuncture (auricular acupressure) is much more effective than psychotherapy in the treatment of competition stress syndrome, and is worth further study.

Substance addiction: The possible use of auricular acupuncture as a treatment for opium dependence was first noted in 1973. Since then it has also been found useful for smoking cessation and alcohol recidivism.

Pediatric disorders: Diarrhea in infants and young children, convulsions due to high fever, cough relief, and may be of some help in the treatment of Tourette syndrome in children.

Disorders of the sense organs: Sudden-onset deafness in adults, Meniere's Disease, tinnitus (ear ringing), unexplained earache, and simple epistaxis (nose bleeds) unassociated with generalized or local disease.

Skin diseases: In some countries, many skin diseases are customarily treated with acupuncture, but very few controlled studies have been published. In a randomized controlled clinical trial on chloasma, acupuncture had a significantly better effect than vitamins C and E. It can benefit Herpes zoster (human (alpha) herpesvirus 3), has an antipruritic effect, and has also been shown to benefit neurodermatitis, and acne vulgaris in research trials.

Cancers: No controlled study has been reported on the efficacy of acupuncture in the treatment of cancer itself. However, acupuncture still has uses in cancer treatments. One is to relieve cancer pain, and the other is to control the adverse reactions to radiotherapy and chemotherapy. For radiotherapy and chemotherapy, acupuncture can greatly lessen the adverse reactions in the digestive and nervous systems, as well as providing protection against damage to hematopoiesis.

Other reports: Obesity and hyperlipemia are becoming increasingly important medical issues. If acupuncture could help in reducing body weight and blood lipids, its clinical use could be greatly expanded. Quite a number of reports on this effect have been published, but unfortunately, almost none of them is methodologically sound. Acupuncture may be of benefit to patients with non-insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus. Its efficacy has been shown to be superior to that of placebos and comparable with that of tolbutamide. Anisodamine is effective in treating excessive salivation induced by drugs (usually antipsychotics), but acupuncture seems to be more effective. There are also reports on the treatment of Sjoegren syndrome (sicca syndrome), Raynaud syndrome, Stein-Leventhal syndrome (polycystic ovary syndrome), and Tietze syndrome (costochondritis), which indicate beneficial effects from acupuncture treatment. Since these reports have appeared only in individual papers, confirmation by further study is necessary.
